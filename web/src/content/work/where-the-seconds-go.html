What I built

An expert cache for llama.cpp, for [[wsg.batch|batch 1]] decode only. On RTX 5090 hosts it ran [[wsg.grid|2.0-4.0×]] stock llama.cpp and faster than FreeToken in [[sec.vs_ft|11 of 12]] cells. gpt-oss-120b ran at [[sec.speed|58-153 tokens/s]] with [[sec.speed|11-40%]] of its experts on the GPU. At gpt-oss's smallest cache on [[sec.limit|25 consumer machines]], a scope I set after seeing the data, it reached [[sec.limit|31-54%]] of a limit computed from my own bandwidth probe, not a physical one.

The model has [[wsg.size|36 MoE layers of 128 experts at 13.25 MB each]], [[wsg.size|about 61 GB]] of experts. An RTX 5090 has [[wsg.size|32 GB]]. So most experts stay in host memory, and each token waits on host reads.

Each MoE layer gets its own GPU slots. A hit runs on the GPU. A miss goes to CPU helper threads through a mailbox the GPU writes, or a per-machine table copies it over PCIe into a slot in the same step. A decayed-frequency rule decides what stays. An admitted miss is read twice: the CPU serves it, then a background copy fills the slot.

The limit is the fewest host reads any cache of that size can make (Belady's MIN with bypass, on the model's own routing trace) at the machine's best probed read rate. I also built oracles into the engine. They read future routing from a recorded trace, so I could change one thing at a time.

Read those ratios with these beside them:

  • The [[wsg.headline|1.29×]] is host B, the faster-memory host. The same cell at stock clock is [[wsg.headline|1.21×]].
  • FreeToken was faster at one cell, [[wsg.slower|0.97×]] at Qwen3-30B-A3B [[wsg.grid|43.75%]] on host S. I didn't separate why.
  • Most of the difference from FreeToken is admission policy. On host S, LRU in place of my rule ran [[wsg.lru|6-21%]] slower, and slower than FreeToken at [[wsg.lru|4 of 6]] cells. I read this as: a policy change in FreeToken could close much of it. I didn't test that.
  • Host S missed a band I registered before renting it. [[wsg.band|5 of 6]] ratios fell outside [[wsg.band|±0.06]] of host B's.
  • With FreeToken tuned at [[wsg.ft_tuned|5 settings per cell]] on a separate Ryzen 9 9950X rental, the ratios are [[wsg.ft_tuned|0.98-1.17×]]. Tuning moved FreeToken [[wsg.ft_tuned|at most 4%]].
  • Stock llama.cpp is a weak baseline. Read the FreeToken column first.

What limited it

The gap is the deployed cache's time above the limit. I split it with the oracles. The panel is exploratory. The new machines were rented for a registered test.

About half of the gap stayed after all three changes, at gpt-oss-120b's 11%. [[wsg.gap]]

With the table on, it puts misses the cache didn't choose into its slots, so MIN's set read twice closes about none of the gap. With the table off, that set closes [[wsg.gap|19%]]. Changing what's cached and how it's read together closes [[wsg.gap|33-36%]]. Read-ahead adds [[wsg.gap|15-19%]]. That leaves [[wsg.gap|43-52%]], and [[wsg.gap|42-48%]] [[wsg.gap|at 25%]]. The GPU's non-expert time is [[wsg.tgpu|2.9 ms]] per token.

Without foresight, four variants built from the engine's own parts recovered [[wsg.online|at most 6%]] of an oracle's gain on [[wsg.online|5 fast-link machines]]. The registered bound there was loose.

I read this as: closing the rest would take reads issued before routing, at MIN's count, and that takes a prediction of the routing.

What it doesn't show

  • Anything above batch 1. The cache only serves single-token steps. At [[wsg.grid|25%]] on host S, prefill ran [[wsg.batch|79-83%]] of stock and decode at [[wsg.batch|2-4 sequences]] ran [[wsg.batch|77-79%]] of stock, one run per configuration.
  • A production part. A GPU wait on a stuck helper aborts the process, after a [[wsg.abort|default of 2 s]]. I treat this as a measurement instrument.
  • A physical limit. The limit is relative to my probe, and it assumes exact routing, whole experts, per-layer slots and one token per step. A reader faster than my probe could go below it. None of [[wsg.under|302 timed runs]] did, and the closest was [[wsg.under|1.33×]] it. A closed-form estimate, not counters, puts the engine's implied read rate [[wsg.implied|more than 5%]] above the probe on [[wsg.implied|12 of 84]] launch-budgets.
  • A registered range. The [[sec.limit|31-54%]] is post hoc and covers gpt-oss's smallest cache only, [[wsg.shares|median 47%]]. At [[wsg.grid|25%]], consumer machines reach [[wsg.shares|23-36%]]. My machine list drops [[wsg.excluded|5 unstable rentals]]. All 5 have server processors, so the drop can only touch the server range. The [[wsg.shares|3 server machines]] left reach [[wsg.shares|12-46%]], and I haven't established why.
  • Other setups. Hosts were rented, a convenience sample. One engine, two models, and AIME prompts I also used in development.
  • Task accuracy. Output checks are teacher-forced KL divergence against an all-VRAM reference. Task accuracy wasn't measured.
  • A clean prediction record. [[wsg.scorecard|142 of 553]] scored prediction clauses failed. All of them are in the scorecard.
  • Review. This is a public manuscript, not peer reviewed. The llama.cpp patch is not upstream.
