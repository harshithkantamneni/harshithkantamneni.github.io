What I built

Kernels

I built Triton forward and backward kernels for stick-breaking attention, merged in #1319. On H100, Nsight Compute points backward cost to fp32 scans at [[pr1319.occupancy|about 12% occupancy]]. That attribution is my reading, because the tool reports stall classes, not scans. On H100, [[pr1319.tiles|64×32 tiles]] fit [[pr1319.tiles|57-89 KiB]] of shared memory where [[pr1319.tiles|64×64]] needed [[pr1319.tiles|129-193 KiB]]. The kernels still take [[pr1319.vs_authors|1.04-1.42×]] the original authors' time on H100. The [[sbk.vs_prev|1.29-1.48×]] gain on H100 is against the PR's own earlier head, mostly from the backward rewrite.

Stick-breaking gives the nearest key a share beta of a stick, the next key beta of what is left, and so on. #1319 continues the unmerged #599. In review, the maintainer found a zero gradient at saturated logits, in bf16 as well as fp16. The fix rewrites it so nothing cancels when beta rounds to one.

Inference

I added KV-cache decoding, merged in #1412 and tested against the uncached forward. On H200, [[pr1412.step|at batch 1]] and [[pr1412.step|64K keys]], [[pr1412.grid|32 programs on 132 SMs]] take [[pr1412.step|4.3 ms]]. Reading that K/V once would take [[pr1412.floor|about 0.06 ms]], computed from the H200's rated bandwidth, not measured. The H200 model-level step sits at [[sbk.model|about 9 ms]], which I did not profile.

The decoding kernel is forward-only and runs one program per query and head, each walking that query's keys nearest first.

On H100, on forward plus backward, the backward rewrite alone gave [[sbk.split|1.27-1.34×]], and the retile and launch order add [[sbk.split|1.02-1.12×]]. On the forward alone, those two give [[sbk.split|1.11-1.82×]]. The retile and launch order cost L2 reuse: in Nsight Compute on H100, the forward's hit rate fell from [[sbk.l2|92%]] to [[sbk.l2|78%]].

On H100, the tests pass [[sbk.tests|99/99]] in two runs, and [[sbk.tests|56 of the 99]] fail on the earlier head. The maintainer's GB200 run also passed. My #1319 timing is H100 only. Saturated-logit coverage reaches [[sbk.tests|15 in fp16]] and [[sbk.tests|30 in bf16]].

At model level on H200, a cached step stays at [[sbk.model|8.98-9.07 ms]] per token at every prompt and batch size I ran. Against recomputing, that ranges from [[sbk.model|0.9×]], at [[sbk.model|B=1 with 512- and 2048-token prompts]], to [[sbk.model|45.5×]]. The model has random weights, and each time is a median of [[sbk.model|3 calls]].

What limited it

On H100, the two backward kernels take 85% of Nsight Compute time across this PR's three kernels, at no more than 35% SM throughput. [[sbk.ncu]]
KernelTimeSM throughput
Forward, before the retile2174 us31%
Forward, this PR1262 us49%
Backward dq, this PR3808 us32%
Backward dkv, this PR3580 us35%

On H100, no pipe saturates in the backward: ALU runs [[sbk.pipes|22-32%]] and XU [[sbk.pipes|14-18%]]. Snapshot traffic isn't the limit: dq's DRAM writes are [[sbk.pipes|about 3%]] of HBM bandwidth, and dkv reads at a [[sbk.pipes|96%]] L2 hit rate. Registers cap occupancy: at [[sbk.occ|255 registers]] per thread, [[sbk.occ|2 CTAs of 4 warps]] fit on an SM, which is [[sbk.occ|8 of 64 warps]], or [[sbk.occ|12.5%]]. The retile did not raise it: CTAs per SM went from [[sbk.occ|1 to 2]], and each CTA from [[sbk.occ|8 warps to 4]]. I read the remaining cost as stalls on dependent fp32 scan and exp2/log2 results. That is inference, not a line-level measurement.

What it doesn't show

  • That these kernels are faster than the authors'. On H100, tensor-core scans made the forward [[sbk.scans|about 3×]] faster than the earlier head, but [[sbk.scans|20 of the 28]] bf16 saturated-logit tests failed, so the scans stay in fp32. The backward also leaves out the authors' spin locks. I did not isolate either cost.
  • Hot-loop latency. do_bench clears L2 before each call, and clocks weren't locked.
  • A measured decode limit. On H200, [[pr1412.grid|32 programs on 132 SMs]] is grid arithmetic, not a counter reading. The read time of [[pr1412.floor|about 0.06 ms]] is a floor no kernel reaches.
  • A cause for the H200 step at [[sbk.model|about 9 ms]]. It stays flat while the op time grows with keys, and I can't reconcile the two.
  • Production decode. It is one H200, forward only, no split-KV. The H200 numbers predate the rebase, which left the kernels byte-identical but changed model code around them. The non-Hopper path ran only in the CPU interpreter.
